    Key findings from the report

    From the report Geospatial job market geography 2026 (research completed Oct 24, 2026, ~130 cited sources; Markdown). Figures below are drawn from the same data files as the map.

    Pay after rent, by hub

    Cartographer/photogrammetrist mean wage (BLS OEWS, May 2023) minus twelve months of rent (Apartment List, Oct 2026). Dot = home price ÷ wage (lower is more affordable; Q1 2026).

    Small core occupations, big adjacent pool

    Universities award ~7,100 geography/GIS credentials a year against ~1,000 openings in the two "pure" geospatial occupations; most graduates land in surveying, planning, software and especially data science, where programming closes a ~$40k pay gap.

    The market is stalled, not sinking. Unemployment 4.1–4.3% since March; payrolls +29k in September; quits at 1.9%; wages trailing inflation. Few layoffs, little hiring.
    Growth is Southern, Mountain and suburban. Only 7 states and 9 of 387 metros grew significantly; Texas, the Carolinas, Utah and Louisiana lead; Sunbelt cores are flat while their suburbs grow.
    Washington is the one big loser. −66,000 metro jobs after 2025 federal cuts; DC and Arlington −4.5%. For civil-agency GIS it is the worst large market in the country right now.
    Geospatial work follows federal money. NGA to St. Louis, Space Command and FBI to Huntsville, USDA to five hubs; NOAA/USGS and civil contractors cut; defense-intel stable.
    For a Missouri graduate: St. Louis first (clearance-gated, cheapest housing), then Denver, DC (cleared only), Huntsville, Raleigh, Austin/Houston, Kansas City. Mizzou, UMSL and Northwest Missouri State hold NGA pipelines; Missouri State does not — route in via GeoSTL, GeoFutures events or a TGI-member graduate program.
    What raises pay: Python/SQL/cloud (geospatial data scientist ~$117k vs ~$75k GIS analyst), a clearance (+$119k average total comp), GISP (+24%), surveying licensure (most openings per incumbent).
