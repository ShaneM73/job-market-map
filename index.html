    Pay after rent, by hub

    Small core occupations, big adjacent pool

    Universities award ~7,100 geography/GIS credentials a year against ~1,000 openings in the two "pure" geospatial occupations; most graduates land in surveying, planning, software and especially data science, where programming closes a ~$40k pay gap.

    The market is stalled, not sinking. Unemployment 4.1–4.3% since March; payrolls +29k in September; quits at 1.9%; wages trailing inflation. Few layoffs, little hiring.
    Growth is Southern, Mountain and suburban. Only 7 states and 9 of 387 metros grew significantly; Texas, the Carolinas, Utah and Louisiana lead; Sunbelt cores are flat while their suburbs grow.
    Washington is the one big loser. −66,000 metro jobs after 2025 federal cuts; DC and Arlington −4.5%. For civil-agency GIS it is the worst large market in the country right now.
    Geospatial work follows federal money. NGA to St. Louis, Space Command and FBI to Huntsville, USDA to five hubs; NOAA/USGS and civil contractors cut; defense-intel stable.
    For a Missouri graduate: St. Louis first (clearance-gated, cheapest housing), then Denver, DC (cleared only), Huntsville, Raleigh, Austin/Houston, Kansas City. Mizzou, UMSL and Northwest Missouri State hold NGA pipelines; Missouri State does not — route in via GeoSTL, GeoFutures events or a TGI-member graduate program.
    What raises pay: Python/SQL/cloud (geospatial data scientist ~$117k vs ~$75k GIS analyst), a clearance (+$119k average total comp), GISP (+24%), surveying licensure (most openings per incumbent).

    Spatial science jobs: United States

    Cartographers and photogrammetrists, the BLS category that holds most GIS analysts, are projected to grow 7% from 2025 to 2035, against 3% for all occupations. The "geographer" title is shrinking, but the work is spreading into titles such as GIS analyst, geospatial developer and imagery analyst.

    Spatial science jobs: Missouri

    Missouri pays a little below the national median for spatial work, with lower living costs. The state's growth center is St. Louis, where the $1.7 billion Next NGA West campus opened in fall 2025 and the region counts more than 10,000 geospatial-related jobs.

    St. Louis · federal

    NGA West

    Opened fall 2025 with about 3,000 staff. Imagery analysis, GEOINT and data science; most roles need a security clearance.

    St. Louis · industry

    GeoSTL ecosystem

    Esri, Enabled Intelligence and defense contractors have opened offices downtown. GeoSTL launched in 2026 to grow the talent pipeline.

    Statewide · government

    MoDOT, DNR, counties

    Survey technician, GIS and right-of-way jobs in district offices. A 2025 MoDOT survey technician posting started at about $41,400.

    Kansas City & Columbia

    Engineering & utilities

    Engineering firms and utilities hire GIS technicians, LiDAR and photogrammetry staff, and survey crews.

    Spatial science jobs: Springfield & Southwest Missouri

    The Springfield metro (Greene, Christian, Webster, Polk and Dallas counties) is a small spatial market: architecture and engineering jobs are 0.9% of local employment. Most openings come from local government, City Utilities, engineering and survey firms, and a few defense contractors, so expect a handful of postings at a time.

    Skills employers ask for

    ArcGIS is still the baseline, but most technical postings now pair it with scripting and databases. In 2025 postings for GIS technologists and technicians analyzed by O*NET, ArcGIS appeared in three of four and Python in about a third.
