United States

Spatial work is growing faster than the economy as a whole. Cartographers and photogrammetrists (the BLS category that holds most GIS analysts) are projected to grow 7% from 2025 to 2035, against 3% for all occupations. The traditional "geographer" title is shrinking, but the work is spreading into titles like GIS analyst, geospatial developer and imagery analyst.

Missouri

Missouri pays slightly below the national median for spatial jobs, but living costs are lower too. The state's real story is St. Louis: the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency opened its $1.7 billion Next NGA West campus there in fall 2025, and the region counts more than 10,000 geospatial-related jobs.

United StatesMissouri

BLS OEWS May 2025. Missouri location quotient for cartographers is 0.65, meaning the occupation is about a third less concentrated here than nationally; for surveyors it is 0.86.

St. Louis · federal

NGA West

New $1.7B western HQ opened Sept–Oct 2025 with roughly 3,000 staff. Imagery analysis, GEOINT, data science; most roles need a security clearance. Partners include UMSL, SLU, WashU and SIUE.

St. Louis · industry

GeoSTL ecosystem

Esri, Enabled Intelligence and many defense contractors have opened offices downtown. Regional groups launched GeoSTL in 2026 to grow the workforce pipeline.

Statewide · government

MoDOT, DNR, counties

Survey technician, GIS and right-of-way roles across district offices. A 2025 MoDOT survey technician posting listed about $41,400 to start.

Kansas City & Columbia

Engineering & utilities

Engineering firms (Burns & McDonnell, Olsson, HNTB) and utilities hire GIS techs, LiDAR/photogrammetry staff and survey crews.

Springfield & Southwest Missouri

The Springfield metro (Greene, Christian, Webster, Polk and Dallas counties) is a small spatial market. Architecture and engineering jobs make up only 0.9% of local employment. Most openings come from local government, City Utilities, engineering and survey firms, and a few defense contractors, so expect a handful of postings at a time rather than dozens.

Where the local jobs are

Local government

City, county & MPO

City of Springfield, Greene and Christian counties, Ozarks Transportation Organization. Parcel, addressing, 911, zoning and planning GIS.

Utilities

City Utilities of Springfield

Electric, gas, water and broadband network mapping. Asset management, Esri Utility Network, field data collection.

Engineering & survey

A/E and survey firms

Civil and survey firms around Springfield, Ozark and Nixa. GNSS, drone mapping, CAD-to-GIS, plat and boundary work.

State & federal

MoDOT SW District, MDC, USGS

Transportation, conservation and water-resource mapping. Postings appear on MoCareers and USAJobs.

Defense & intelligence

Contractors

Cartographic and imagery analyst roles (for example Bridge Defense). Clearance often required. Fort Leonard Wood is about 90 miles northeast.

Education & research

Missouri State, OTC, Drury

Lab, research and teaching roles; also the main local source of new GIS graduates and interns.

Reading the local market. Springfield has a low concentration of mapping technicians (LQ 0.40) but typical pay close to the state level after cost of living. Joplin, Branson and the SW Missouri nonmetro area add a few utility, county and survey roles. Many local candidates also apply to remote GIS jobs and to St. Louis or Kansas City.

Skills employers ask for

Esri ArcGIS is still the baseline, but most technical postings now pair it with scripting and databases. In 2025 job postings for GIS technologists and technicians analyzed by the U.S. Department of Labor's O*NET, ArcGIS appeared in three of four postings and Python in about a third.

Tier 1 · required almost everywhere

Core GIS

  • ArcGIS Pro (QGIS as a plus)
  • Cartography and map layout
  • Coordinate systems and projections
  • Data cleaning, QA/QC, metadata
  • Spatial analysis (overlay, proximity, geocoding)
Tier 2 · separates candidates

Technical

  • Python / ArcPy and the ArcGIS API for Python
  • SQL, PostgreSQL / PostGIS, enterprise geodatabases
  • ArcGIS Online / Enterprise, dashboards, Experience Builder
  • Field Maps and Survey123
  • FME, JavaScript web mapping, Git
Tier 3 · specialty

Sensing & measurement

  • Remote sensing and image classification
  • LiDAR and photogrammetry; drone (Part 107) mapping
  • GNSS / total station surveying
  • AutoCAD / Civil 3D and CAD-to-GIS conversion
  • GeoAI, machine learning, cloud processing
Everywhere

Professional

  • Explaining results to non-specialists
  • Problem framing and judging data fitness
  • Attention to detail and positional accuracy
  • Project and time management
  • Validating AI or automated output
